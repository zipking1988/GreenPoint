Three correct weed predictions do not establish reliable weed recognition. Low recall makes automatic treatment inappropriate.

20 held-out images. Every annotated plant counted.

We fixed five development images and twenty images from the official PhenoBench validation split before inference. We compared tight-crop baseline, same-source context, and context with a known development crop reference. The baseline won the predeclared development criterion: weed F1 minus twice the crop-as-weed rate, then precision and simplicity. Evaluation labels were used only to score the frozen pipeline.

Precision is based on only three positive predictions. No confidence interval or farm-level accuracy claim is implied. Missed and uncertain weeds remain in the recall denominator. Boxes are greedily matched one-to-one at intersection-over-union ≥0.5. Partial border instances are included. This is a small custom check, not the official PhenoBench benchmark or mask-quality leaderboard.

What the comparison changed

On development data, baseline and context-only predicted no weeds. The reference strategy predicted six weeds: two correct weeds and four crops. Its crop-as-weed rate was 4/40 (10%), with one incomplete classification response retained as a partial image. That is why references remain an optional experimental comparison and the baseline is the default. We did not tune the model using the twenty evaluation images.

Real moving video: integration, without a tracking claim

Two genuine four-second excerpts from the Bonn Sugar Beets 2016 robot-camera video ran through SAM native video. Both returned zero candidates. This is not evidence of weed-free ground or successful identity tracking. The source has per-frame semantic annotations, but its object keys are not reused across frames; it cannot provide an identity-tracking benchmark.
